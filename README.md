# Quantum-image-encryption-algorithm
The accelerated growth of consumer imaging devices and the approaching quantum computing era has generated a demand for picture encryption frameworks that are both robustly secure and tailored for quantum application. Prevailing quantum-inspired picture encryption approaches often utilize individual chaotic maps or constrained permutations, but they commonly do not offer clear quantum circuit configurations and integrated frameworks for overall security. To address these limitations, this work proposes a quantum-inspired image encryption framework that integrates a four-dimensional memristive hyperchaotic system with plaintext-dependent key derivation, reversible spatial scrambling, nonlinear diffusion, and quantum P-box permutation within a unified architecture. The secret image key, nonce, and plaintext-binding hash are jointly employed to derive independent stage-specific chaotic initial conditions, thereby generating distinct control sequences for the scrambling, diffusion, and P-box stages and strengthening plaintext and key sensitivity. The input image is represented using the generalized novel enhanced quantum representation (GNEQR), followed by a keyed rectangular dual Baker map that performs reversible spatial scrambling over the coordinate register. A chaotic XOR-based diffusion stage subsequently modifies pixel intensities, while a dynamically generated P-box introduces an additional permutation layer to further disrupt positional relationships. The corresponding reversible quantum operators and circuit structures are formulated for the principal encryption stages, and ESOP-based Boolean minimization is incorporated to reduce the realization complexity of the resulting quantum circuits. The dynamical characteristics and statistical properties of the proposed chaotic source are examined through standard nonlinear and randomness analyses, while the encryption performance is evaluated using histogram distribution, information entropy, adjacent-pixel correlation, differential sensitivity, key sensitivity, and robustness tests. The obtained results demonstrate that the proposed framework provides strong confusion and diffusion characteristics, high sensitivity to both plaintext and secret-key variations, exact reversibility, and a structured pathway toward efficient quantum-circuit realization, making it a promising architecture for secure quantum-inspired image protection.
